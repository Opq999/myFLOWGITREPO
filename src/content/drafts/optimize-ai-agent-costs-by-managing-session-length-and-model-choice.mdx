## What you'll get
Reduced operational costs for your AI agent or assistant. This approach works by actively managing the length of your AI sessions and choosing the most cost-effective models for routine tasks, preventing unnecessary resource consumption, especially the "re-read tax" from long conversation histories.

## Tools you need
*   **Your AI Assistant (e.g., ChatGPT, Claude, Gemini)** (freemium): The AI tool you use for tasks, such as a chat interface or a custom agent.

## Steps
1.  **Identify long-running sessions**: The author found that sessions with many messages (e.g., 600+ messages, 7MB transcript) were the biggest cost drivers. While you might not have an audit script, you can manually identify conversations that have grown very long over time.
    *   What to do: Review your active AI assistant conversations or tabs. Look for sessions that have been open for a long time or have accumulated many turns.
    *   What you should see: A list of your current conversations, some of which are noticeably longer than others.

2.  **Archive and restart long sessions**: Implement a "hygiene rule" to regularly close and archive long sessions, starting fresh ones with a brief summary of the previous context.
    *   What to do: For any long-running session you identified, copy the essential context (e.g., the goal, key decisions, or current task status) into a new, fresh chat or session. Then, close or archive the old, long session.
    *   Example handoff prompt:

    ```
    We were working on [briefly describe the project or task]. The last key outcome was [mention last important result]. Continue from here.
    ```

    *   What you should see: A new, short session ready to continue your work, and the old, long session closed or archived. This prevents the AI from re-reading a massive history on every turn.

3.  **Pin routine tasks to cheaper models**: Ensure that automated or routine tasks use the least expensive AI model capable of doing the job.
    *   What to do: If your AI agent or platform allows you to select different models (e.g., a "fast" vs. "smart" model, or different API models like GPT-3.5 vs. GPT-4), review your routine workflows. For tasks that don't require the most advanced reasoning, switch to a cheaper, faster model.
    *   What you should see: Your routine tasks running on a more cost-effective model. The author doesn't share specific model names or how to "pin" them; this step is generic based on the principle.

## Original source
This workflow is inspired by "I trained my AI agent to burn less money. Here's what actually worked." by jenatechio, published on the DEV Community blog. The author shared insights from an audit of their AI assistant's operational costs, revealing that long session histories, not failed tool calls, were the primary driver of expense.

## Notes & variations
*   **Free-tier viability**: Even if you're on a free tier, managing session length can help you avoid hitting usage limits faster, as longer conversations consume more of the provider's resources.
*   **Common mistake**: Assuming that only "failed" operations cost money. The author found that the "re-read tax" (the cost of the AI processing its own long history) was a far greater expense, even when nothing was failing.
*   **Tip for better results**: Make session hygiene a regular habit. Set a reminder to review and archive old sessions weekly or whenever a conversation feels like it's getting too long. This prevents costs from silently accumulating.
