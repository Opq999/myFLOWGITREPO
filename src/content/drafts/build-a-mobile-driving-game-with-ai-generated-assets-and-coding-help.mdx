## Words you'll see, explained
*   **Game Engine**: Software used to create video games, handling graphics, physics, sound, and user input.
*   **3D Model**: A mathematical representation of a three-dimensional object, used in computer graphics for games, animations, or simulations.
*   **Low Poly**: A 3D model that uses a small number of polygons (flat surfaces) to create its shape. This often improves performance in games and can also be a stylistic choice.
*   **AI-generated assets**: Images, models, textures, or other game elements that are created or assisted by artificial intelligence.
*   **Driving mechanics**: The physics and controls that govern how a vehicle moves, accelerates, brakes, and turns within a game.

## Notes & variations
*   **Do you even need this?**: While AI can significantly accelerate parts of game development, especially asset creation and coding assistance, learning basic 3D modeling (e.g., with free tools like Blender) and fundamental game development principles (e.g., through Godot tutorials) remains highly valuable. For simpler 2D games, AI might be less critical, and traditional methods can be more straightforward. Relying solely on cutting-edge, unproven AI tools can sometimes lead to more complex debugging or less control over the final product.
*   **Free-tier limits**: ChatGPT offers a freemium model with usage limits, and the author mentioned using a "Pro plan." Godot Engine is entirely free and open-source. Many AI 3D generation tools, if available, often operate on a credit system or have paid tiers for advanced features or higher usage.
*   **Common pitfall**: Over-reliance on AI for complex tasks without understanding the underlying principles can lead to hard-to-debug issues or generic results. AI-generated assets may also require significant cleanup, optimization, or artistic refinement to fit your game's specific style and performance needs.
