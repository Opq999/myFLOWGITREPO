## Words you'll see, explained
*   **GPU (Graphics Processing Unit)**: A specialized computer chip designed to handle complex calculations quickly, essential for running AI models and generating images or videos.
*   **Node-based interface**: A visual way to build complex workflows by connecting different functional blocks (nodes) together, often used in advanced AI art tools like ComfyUI.
*   **LoRA (Low-Rank Adaptation)**: A small add-on file that modifies a larger AI model to generate images or videos in a specific style or with particular characteristics.
*   **2s and 3s animation**: A traditional animation term referring to how many frames a drawing is held for. "2s" means a drawing is held for 2 frames, "3s" for 3 frames, creating a specific motion feel.
*   **Denoise**: A setting in AI image generation that controls how much the AI tries to clean up or refine the generated image, often affecting detail and style.
*   **Latent Upscaler**: A technique that increases the resolution of an image or video while it's still in a compressed "latent" form, often preserving more detail than traditional upscaling.
*   **Motion smearing**: A visual artifact where fast-moving objects appear blurred or stretched across multiple frames, common in AI-generated video.

## Notes & variations
*   **Do you even need this?**: For simpler anime-style images or short clips, consider using more accessible freemium web-based AI image generators (like Midjourney or Leonardo.ai) that offer anime styles without requiring a complex local setup or powerful GPU. These tools are much easier for beginners.
*   **Free-tier limits**: Running advanced AI animation workflows locally requires a powerful GPU, which is a significant upfront cost. Cloud GPU services offer hourly rates but can quickly become expensive for longer animations. Free tiers for such services are usually very limited in time or processing power.
*   **Common pitfall**: Relying solely on text prompts for complex framing or specific visual outcomes. The author stresses that visual guides (sketches) provide far more control and better results than trying to describe every detail in words.
*   **Tip for better results**: Always prioritize the quality and consistency of your reference images. Using truly anime-style references with flat shading and clean lines is key to achieving an authentic 2D anime look and avoiding the "AI-3D" aesthetic.
