## What you'll get
You will get a first draft of a presentation deck, based on your meeting notes. This approach uses AI to quickly turn raw meeting transcripts into a structured presentation, saving you from starting with a blank page and retyping information.

## Steps
1.  **Record your meeting and get a transcript**: Use a meeting assistant tool like Granola, Fireflies, or Fathom to record your online meeting. After the meeting, these tools will automatically provide a transcript and often a summary. You should have a full transcript of your meeting available in your chosen tool.
2.  **Connect Gamma to your meeting notes**: Go to Gamma (gamma.app) and start a new presentation. Look for an option to "Import" or "Connect" content. The author mentions "connectors," so there should be a way to link Gamma to your meeting assistant tool (Granola, Fireflies, or Fathom) using your own permissions. You should see Gamma successfully linked to your meeting notes source.
3.  **Instruct Gamma to draft the presentation**: Once connected, tell Gamma to create a presentation from your meeting notes. The author specifically asks it to extract "decisions and open questions only." The author doesn't share their exact prompt; a starting point:

    ```
    Create a presentation deck from these meeting notes. Focus only on the key decisions made and any open questions that need follow-up.
    ```

    Gamma will then process the notes and generate a first draft of your presentation.
4.  **Review and refine the presentation**: Gamma will present you with a draft deck. The author notes it will likely be generic. Review the slides, especially the opener. The author suggests rewriting the opener yourself to make it more specific and engaging. You should have a presentation draft that you can now edit and customize, saving you the effort of starting from scratch.

## Notes & variations
*   **Free-tier alternatives**: Granola, Fireflies, Fathom, and Gamma all offer free tiers that are suitable for occasional use or shorter meetings. Check their specific limits for meeting duration or number of presentations.
*   **Common mistake**: Expecting a perfect, ready-to-share presentation from the first AI draft. The AI provides a starting point; your human touch is still needed for clarity, tone, and specific details.
*   **Tip for better results**: Before generating, quickly skim your meeting notes and highlight the most critical decisions or questions yourself. This can help you refine your prompt to Gamma for a more focused first draft.
