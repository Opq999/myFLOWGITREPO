## What this is, in plain English
This workflow shows how an advanced AI assistant, ChatGPT Work with its powerful GPT-6 Astra (Max) model, can create custom running routes. You provide your address and desired distance, and the AI generates detailed routes that loop back to your starting point. It uses geographic data from OpenStreetMap (OSM) and internal tools like Nominatim and Overpass to understand locations and map data.

This is a 'Concept mode' entry because the exact steps and code the AI uses internally are not visible or reproducible by the user. This means you interact with the AI using a simple prompt, but the complex process behind it remains a 'black box'.

Essentially, it's a demonstration of a highly capable AI agent performing a complex task by orchestrating multiple tools and data sources behind the scenes, without exposing the technical details to the user.

## How it actually works
This workflow relies on the advanced capabilities of ChatGPT Work with the GPT-6 Astra (Max) model, which acts as an intelligent agent. The user provides a natural language prompt, and the AI interprets it, then orchestrates a series of internal steps to fulfill the request.

1.  **Open ChatGPT Work**: Access the ChatGPT Work interface.
2.  **Provide your prompt**: Type a clear request specifying your address, desired route distances (e.g., 5K, 10K), and that the routes should loop from your house. The author's prompt was similar to this:

    ```
    I live at <my address>. Figure out 5K and 10K running routes from me that loop from my house. Use OSM data.
    ```

3.  **Wait for the AI to process**: The AI will then internally use tools like Nominatim to locate your address and Overpass to download relevant OpenStreetMap (OSM) data for roads and trails in your area. It calculates the loops and generates the routes. The author reported that this process took about 27 minutes.
4.  **Review the results**: ChatGPT Work will display the generated routes as an embedded visualization (a map) and provide downloadable GPX and GeoJSON files.

It's important to note that the exact code or detailed steps the AI performs internally are not visible to the user, as the author experienced. The system handles the technical execution behind the scenes.

## Words you'll see, explained
*   **GPX file**: A common file format for GPS data, used for sharing routes, tracks, and waypoints, often imported into running watches or apps.
*   **GeoJSON file**: A standard format for encoding geographic data structures, commonly used for representing points, lines, and polygons on web maps.
*   **Nominatim**: A tool used for searching OpenStreetMap data by name and address, converting human-readable locations into geographic coordinates (geocoding).
*   **Overpass**: A powerful API (Application Programming Interface) for querying and retrieving specific data from the OpenStreetMap database.
*   **OpenStreetMap (OSM)**: A free, editable map of the world, created and maintained by a global community of volunteers.
*   **LLM (Large Language Model)**: An AI program trained on vast amounts of text to understand, generate, and respond to human-like language.

## Notes & variations
*   **Do you even need this?**: For basic route planning, simpler alternatives like Google Maps, Strava, or other dedicated running apps can help. However, these might not offer the same level of custom loop generation from an arbitrary address or direct export of GPX/GeoJSON files as easily as an advanced AI agent can.
*   **Free-tier limits**: ChatGPT Work with GPT-6 Astra (Max) is likely a premium or paid offering, meaning it may not be accessible through free tiers of standard ChatGPT. Access to such advanced agent capabilities typically requires a paid subscription or specific enterprise access.
*   **Common pitfall**: Being too vague in your prompt. While the AI is powerful, providing a precise address and clear requirements (e.g., "loop from my house," "5K and 10K") will yield much better and more accurate results.
