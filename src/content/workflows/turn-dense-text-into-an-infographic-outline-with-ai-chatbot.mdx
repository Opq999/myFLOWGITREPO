## What you'll get
You will get a clear, structured outline for a single infographic, even from very dense text. This outline includes the one main point a viewer should remember, 3 to 5 key sections with facts, suggestions for visual forms, and a list of content that was cut. This approach helps you focus your message before you start designing, solving the common problem of trying to fit too many ideas into one graphic.

## Tools you need
*   **AI Chatbot (freemium)**: To generate the structured infographic outline from your text. Examples include ChatGPT, Claude, or Gemini.
*   **Canva (freemium)**: To design the visual infographic based on the outline you create.
*   **Gamma (freemium)**: An alternative tool to Canva for designing presentations and infographics.

## Steps
1.  **Open your AI Chatbot**: Go to a freemium AI chatbot like ChatGPT (chatgpt.com), Claude (claude.ai), or Gemini (gemini.google.com) on your phone or computer.

2.  **Prepare your text**: Have the dense text ready that you want to turn into an infographic. This could be a few paragraphs from an article, a report, or a business document.

3.  **Paste the prompt**: Copy and paste the exact prompt below into the chatbot. This prompt tells the AI exactly how to structure your information:

    ```
    I will paste a block of text. Do not summarize it. Turn it into the skeleton of a single infographic. State the ONE thing a viewer should remember. If the text has more than one, tell me it needs to be more than one graphic and stop. Propose 3 to 5 sections max. Each section = a short header (max 5 words) and the single stat or fact that earns its place. Cut everything that does not support the one takeaway. For each section, say what visual form fits: number, comparison, sequence, or simple icon list. Do not default everything to a bar chart. List what you had to drop. I want to see what got cut so I can argue with it.
    ```

    You should see the chatbot acknowledge the prompt and wait for your text input.

4.  **Add your text**: Immediately after the prompt (or when the chatbot asks for it), paste your dense text. Press Enter or Send.

    You should get a structured outline that includes a single main takeaway, 3 to 5 sections with short headers and key facts, suggestions for visual forms (like numbers, comparisons, sequences, or icon lists), and a list of content the AI decided to cut.

5.  **Review and refine**: Read through the AI's output. Pay close attention to the "List what you had to drop" section. This part is crucial because it highlights information the AI thought was less relevant to the main takeaway, helping you understand the core message better. If the AI said the text needs more than one graphic, consider if you can simplify your original text or create multiple infographics.

6.  **Design your infographic**: Once you are happy with the outline, open Canva or Gamma. Use the structured outline to create your visual infographic, focusing on the main takeaway and using the suggested visual forms for each section.

## Notes & variations
*   **Common mistake**: A common pitfall is trying to force too many ideas into one infographic. The prompt is designed to help you identify the single most important takeaway. If the AI tells you the text has more than one main idea, it's a sign that you might need to create multiple infographics or simplify your source material.
*   **Tip for better results**: Before using the prompt, quickly read your text and try to identify what you think the single most important message is. This will help you evaluate the AI's output and guide any refinements. The "cut list" is your friend: use it to challenge the AI's choices and ensure the final outline truly reflects your intended message.
*   **Free-tier alternatives**: The AI chatbots mentioned (ChatGPT, Claude, Gemini) all offer robust free tiers that are sufficient for this workflow. Canva and Gamma also have free versions that allow you to create basic infographics.
