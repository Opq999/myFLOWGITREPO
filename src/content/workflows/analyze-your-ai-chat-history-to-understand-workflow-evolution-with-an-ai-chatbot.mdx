## What you'll get
You will receive an AI-generated analysis of how your personal AI usage and workflow have changed over time, based on your past chat transcripts. This approach works because the AI can process a large volume of your historical interactions, spotting patterns and evolutions in your workflow that might be hard to notice yourself.

## Tools you need
*   **AI Chatbot** (freemium): A conversational AI tool like ChatGPT, Claude, or Gemini, used to analyze your past conversations.

## Steps

1.  **Gather your chat transcripts**: Collect all your raw conversation transcripts from your past AI chats. The author used 63 transcripts. Make sure they are in chronological order, from oldest to newest.
    You should have a collection of text files or copied chat histories, ready to paste.

2.  **Start a new chat thread**: Open your chosen AI chatbot (for example, ChatGPT, Claude, or Gemini) and start a brand new conversation. This ensures the AI has a fresh context window (its memory of the current conversation) and isn't influenced by previous unrelated chats.
    You should see an empty chat window, ready for your input.

3.  **Feed the transcripts chronologically**: Paste each of your raw conversation transcripts into the new chat thread, one by one, in the order they happened. The author did this "with as little explanation as possible," meaning they just pasted the text without adding extra comments.
    You should see the AI chatbot receiving each transcript as a separate message.

4.  **Ask the AI to analyze your workflow evolution**: After pasting all transcripts, ask the AI to describe how your use of AI changed over time, using evidence from the early, middle, and late parts of the archive.

    ```
    Based on the chat transcripts I've provided, describe how my use of AI changed over time. Use evidence from the early, middle, and late parts of the archive to support your observations.
    ```

    You should get an analysis from the AI, potentially highlighting shifts like "early broad generation, then more human direction and rejection, different models getting different jobs."

5.  **Challenge the AI's analysis (optional but recommended)**: Ask the AI to find areas where its initial developmental story might be "too neat" or oversimplified. This helps uncover deeper insights or contradictions in your workflow evolution.

    ```
    Find three places where that developmental story was probably too neat or oversimplified. Provide evidence from the archive for each.
    ```

    You should see the AI identifying nuances or contradictions, such as an earlier instinct to "upload everything" during continuity problems, which later evolved into selective context transfer.

## Notes & variations
*   **Free-tier alternatives**: You can use the free tiers of popular AI chatbots like ChatGPT (chatgpt.com), Claude (claude.ai), or Gemini (gemini.google.com). Be aware of context window limits, which might prevent you from pasting all 63 transcripts at once. If this happens, you might need to analyze smaller batches of transcripts separately.
*   **Common mistake**: A common pitfall is hitting the AI's context window limit, especially with many transcripts. If the AI stops remembering earlier parts of your conversation, it means you've given it too much text. Try breaking your input into smaller, themed chunks or using a paid tier with a larger context window.
*   **Tip for better results**: Be clear and specific in your prompts. If the initial analysis isn't detailed enough, ask follow-up questions like "Can you elaborate on the shift from broad generation to human direction?" or "Show me specific examples from the transcripts for that point."
