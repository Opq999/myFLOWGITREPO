## What this is, in plain English
This entry explains a powerful way to structure your requests (called "prompts") to AI tools like ChatGPT. It's designed to get much better, more useful, and accurate answers for almost any task you can imagine, from writing to research, coding, and planning.

The original author shared a specific prompt template, but the full text of that template was not included in the source material. This means we cannot provide the exact copy-paste steps for that specific prompt.

Instead, this entry focuses on the *ideas* behind the prompt, showing you the key elements that make it effective. You can use these principles to build your own powerful prompts or understand what makes a good prompt work.

While the concept of prompt engineering (designing effective prompts) can be complex, the author designed their universal prompt to be easy for beginners to use once they have the full template.

## What you can use it for
*   **Improve writing**: Get help drafting emails, articles, social media posts, or creative stories with better structure and tone.
*   **Conduct research**: Ask for summaries of complex topics, explanations of concepts, or key facts from a specific field.
*   **Plan projects**: Get assistance in outlining project steps, brainstorming ideas, or creating schedules.
*   **Make decisions**: Ask the AI to weigh pros and cons, analyze options, or provide different perspectives on a choice.
*   **Learn new skills**: Request explanations of technical concepts, coding examples, or step-by-step guides for various tasks.

## How it actually works
This method involves using a single, well-structured prompt at the start of a conversation with an AI. This prompt sets the stage for all future interactions by guiding the AI on how to respond. The author describes the prompt as covering several key layers:

1.  **Define the AI's role**: The prompt begins by telling the AI *who* it should be (e.g., "an expert assistant with deep knowledge"). This helps the AI adopt a helpful persona and respond appropriately.
2.  **Provide your context**: You would then share information about yourself ("Who I am"), your overall goal ("What I need"), and any relevant background ("Any relevant context"). This helps the AI tailor its responses to your specific situation.
3.  **Specify output format**: Crucially, the prompt includes instructions on *how* you want the AI's answer formatted ("How I want the output"), such as a bulleted list, a specific tone, or a certain length.
4.  **Frame each task**: For each new request, you would then clearly state the specific task you want the AI to perform ("TASK") and optionally provide an example of what you're looking for ("EXAMPLE"). This ensures the AI understands your immediate request within the broader context.
5.  **Guard against errors**: The prompt also includes instructions to prevent the AI from "inventing facts" (known as "hallucinations") and to "push back if something I'm asking for is a bad idea," ensuring more reliable results.

## Words you'll see, explained
*   **AI (Artificial Intelligence)**: Computer systems designed to perform tasks that normally require human intelligence, like understanding language or recognizing patterns.
*   **Prompt**: The instructions or questions you give to an AI model to get a specific response.
*   **Prompt Engineering**: The skill of designing effective prompts to get the best possible results from AI models.
*   **Hallucination**: When an AI model generates information that is false, nonsensical, or not supported by its training data, presenting it as fact.
*   **Context**: Background information or details you provide to the AI that help it understand your request better and give more relevant answers.

## Notes & variations
*   **Do you even need this?**: For very simple, one-off questions, you might not need such a detailed prompt. A direct question can often work fine. However, for complex tasks or when you need consistent, high-quality output, using a structured prompt like this can significantly improve results.
*   **Free-tier limits**: ChatGPT's free tier (GPT-3.5) is generally sufficient for experimenting with prompt structures like this. More advanced models (GPT-4) available in paid tiers often follow complex instructions better, but the principles apply to both.
*   **Common pitfall**: A common mistake is not being specific enough in your `TASK` or `OUTPUT FORMAT` sections. The more clearly you tell the AI what to do and how to present it, the better the outcome will be.
