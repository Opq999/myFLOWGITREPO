## What you'll get
You will get a clear assessment of whether a text message, email, link, or online conversation is a scam. The AI will explain why it looks legitimate or suspicious, if it matches a known scam pattern, and what you should do next (block, report, or ignore). This approach works because it combines the AI's understanding with real-time threat intelligence from cybersecurity experts, helping to identify new and evolving scam patterns quickly.

## Tools you need
*   **ChatGPT** (freemium): The popular AI chatbot from OpenAI.
*   **Claude** (freemium): An AI chatbot from Anthropic, known for its long context window.
*   **Norton Genie** (free): A free AI-powered scam detection tool, used here as a plugin or connector within AI chatbots.
*   **Malwarebytes** (free): A cybersecurity company offering tools to protect against malware and scams, used here as a plugin or connector.

## Steps
1.  **Choose your AI Chatbot**: Decide whether you want to use ChatGPT or Claude for this task. Both offer free tiers and can integrate the scam detection tools.
    *   **What to do**: Open your preferred AI chatbot in your web browser or mobile app.
    *   **What you should see**: The main chat interface of ChatGPT or Claude.

2.  **Add the scam detection plugin or connector**: You need to add either Norton Genie or Malwarebytes to your chosen chatbot. The author states these are free to use within the chatbots.
    *   **What to do (ChatGPT)**: In the ChatGPT interface, look for the "Apps" or "Plugins" section (usually in the sidebar). Search for "Norton Genie" or "Malwarebytes" and add it.
    *   **What to do (Claude)**: In the Claude interface, go to "Settings" (often a gear icon) and then find "Connectors." Search for "Norton Genie" or "Malwarebytes" and add it.
    *   **What you should see**: A confirmation that the plugin or connector has been successfully added or enabled.

3.  **Activate the plugin or connector for your chat**: Before asking your question, make sure the scam detection tool is active for the conversation.
    *   **What to do**: In ChatGPT, you might need to select the plugin from a dropdown menu above the chat input box. In Claude, once a connector is added, it's usually available to be selected or automatically used when relevant. Start a new chat if you're unsure.
    *   **What you should see**: An indication that the Norton Genie or Malwarebytes plugin/connector is active for your current chat session.

4.  **Paste your suspicious content and ask for a check**: Copy the exact message, link, or conversation you want to check. The more detail you provide, the better the AI can analyze it.
    *   **What to do (for suspicious messages)**: Paste the message into the chat and use this prompt:

    ```
    Is this a scam: [paste the message exactly as you got it, or upload a screenshot] Tell me specifically what makes it look legitimate or suspicious, whether it matches a known scam pattern, and what I should do next, block, report, or ignore.
    ```

    *   **What to do (for suspicious links)**: Paste the link into the chat and use this prompt:

    ```
    Check this link before I click it: [paste it]. Is this a real site, a lookalike, or something dangerous?
    ```

    *   **What to do (for suspicious marketplace conversations)**: Paste the conversation into the chat and use this prompt:

    ```
    Here's a conversation with someone I'm buying from: [paste the messages]. Does anything here match a common marketplace scam, and how do I respond safely?
    ```

    *   **What you should see**: A detailed response from the AI, powered by the scam detection tool, explaining its findings and recommending a course of action.

5.  **Understand the AI's response**: Pay close attention to the AI's assessment, especially if it indicates uncertainty.
    *   **What to do**: Read the AI's explanation carefully. If the response comes back as "uncertain," treat it as a warning to be cautious, not as a sign that it's probably safe.
    *   **What you should see**: A clear understanding of the potential risk and the recommended next steps.

## Notes & variations
*   **Free-tier alternative**: If you prefer not to use an AI chatbot, Norton Genie also offers a standalone free web tool at `genie.norton.com` where you can paste suspicious content directly for analysis.
*   **Common mistake**: A frequent error is to assume an "uncertain" result means the content is probably safe. The author explicitly warns that "uncertain" means the AI hasn't seen a categorised pattern yet, and you should treat it as a signal to be careful, not to proceed.
*   **Tip for better results**: Always provide the complete, unedited text or a clear screenshot of the suspicious message, link, or conversation. Any missing details could lead to a less accurate assessment from the AI.
